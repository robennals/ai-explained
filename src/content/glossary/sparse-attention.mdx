**Sparse attention** is running full attention over only a small, chosen subset of earlier words instead of the entire context.

Full attention compares every word to every other word, so its cost grows with the square of the context length. Sparse attention keeps the whole window available but uses a small, cheap model, an indexer, to score every earlier word for relevance to the word currently being generated. Full attention then runs only on the top-scoring shortlist, no matter how long the document gets.

Some examples:

- **DeepSeek's DSA (DeepSeek Sparse Attention)** — uses a lightning indexer to keep the top 2,048 words out of a context window that can hold 128,000, then runs full attention on only those 2,048.
- **GLM-5.2's IndexShare** — reuses one indexer shortlist across several neighboring layers instead of recomputing it at every layer.

Sparse attention is what makes a million-word context window practical: scoring every word is cheap, and running full attention on only the shortlist avoids the trillion-comparison cost that full attention would otherwise need.
