A **loss** is a number that says how far a [model](/glossary#model) is from perfect, measured across a set of examples (the [training data](/glossary#training-data)).

Some examples:

- **A runner over 10 races** — each race has its own error. The runner's loss is the average of those 10 errors: a verdict on the runner, not on any single race.
- **A photo classifier on 10 000 photos** — each photo contributes one error. The classifier's loss is the average; a loss of 0.05 means the classifier is confidently right almost everywhere.
- **A next-word predictor on a billion sentences** — each position contributes one error (`−log(probability of the right word)`). The predictor's loss is the average across every position. This is the number [training](/glossary#training) shrinks.

The goal of [training](/glossary#training) is to find [parameter](/glossary#parameter) values that minimise the loss on the training data (the **training loss**), typically using [gradient descent](/glossary#gradient-descent). Loss measured on data the model didn't train on is the **test loss** — the two often differ, since a model can memorise its training data and still flunk on fresh examples.
