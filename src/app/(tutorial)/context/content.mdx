# Getting the Right Information

<Lead>
Models can only see so much at once. This chapter looks at how to make the most of that limited window, and how to fill it with the information that actually matters.
</Lead>

## The Wall

<div>
In [Chapter 7](/attention), attention let every word look at every other word, and decide how much to listen to each one. That is what makes attention so good at connecting distant words: a pronoun on page ten can still reach back to the name it refers to on page one. But comparing every word to every other word has a cost, and that cost grows fast.

Double the number of words in the context, and the number of pairs to compare quadruples. Ten times the words means a hundred times the comparisons. This is quadratic growth, and it applies separately to every layer of the model, and again for every new word it generates.

A page of text is a few hundred words. A book is tens of thousands. A large codebase can run past a million lines. Push the context out to a million words, and full attention needs on the order of a trillion comparisons, per layer, per new word generated. Yet models today read whole books and codebases in one go. Something has to give.
</div>

## Don't Redo the Past

<div>
A model generates one word at a time. Each new word attends back over every word that came before it, including the words it just generated itself. Ask for a hundred words of output, and the last word needs the keys and values of all ninety-nine before it.

Recomputing those keys and values from scratch at every step is wasteful. The keys and values for a word never change once that word is written, so a model can compute them once and store them: the KV cache. Generating the next word then only costs a single new set of keys and values, reused against everything already stored.

The catch is memory. The cache holds one entry per word in the prompt plus every word generated so far, and it only grows. That growing cache is the real cost of a long context, and it shapes several of the tricks in the rest of this chapter.
</div>

<KVCacheWidget>
Generate a dozen words with reuse off, then on. Watch how without the cache the model redoes all its earlier work every single step, while the cache turns that into one small step per word.
</KVCacheWidget>
